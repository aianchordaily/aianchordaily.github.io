Myth vs Fact #008 · November 26, 2026

"Home robots are almost here"? AI can walk a robot. It still can't load your dishwasher.

TL;DR A new independent benchmark has AI models control a simulated humanoid robot through household tasks. Every model could navigate, but none reliably loaded a dishwasher or did laundry, so the "robot butler" is still some way off.

The story

Artificial Analysis, an independent AI evaluation company, released AA-Robotics-Sim v0.1, a research-preview benchmark. Frontier language models write code to control a simulated Unitree G1 humanoid robot in NVIDIA Isaac Sim across 10 household and industrial tasks, such as playing a toy piano, loading a dishwasher, watering plants and navigating a warehouse. Each model practices for 5 episodes, keeps only its own notes and code, and is then scored on 10 new scenes per task.

Nine models were tested. GPT-6.2 Astra led at 71.0, ahead of GPT-6.2 Sol at its maximum setting (62.6) and Claude Fable 5.2 (57.5). Walking is effectively solved, since every model scored 100 on navigation, but grabbing and handling objects is not: no model reliably loaded the dishwasher and none finished the laundry.

Separately, a new paper, Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test, put mirrors into robot test scenes. Every robot policy the authors tested performed worse, with average success drops of 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points depending on the policy (author-reported).

Why it matters

Robot demo videos make it look like household help is around the corner. Independent tests tell a more grounded story: moving around is getting easy, but manipulation, meaning picking things up, holding them gently and placing them exactly, is still the hard part. So are everyday surprises like mirrors and shiny surfaces.

Caveats: AA-Robotics-Sim is simulation only, with 100 evaluation episodes per model, and its authors describe it as a preview. The mirror paper's numbers are author-reported.

What this means for you

  • Discount "robot butler next year" marketing. Ask whether a robot can handle objects reliably, not just walk.
  • Expect robots in structured places first, like warehouses and factories, before messy kitchens.
  • Your hands' skills are harder to automate than they look. That's good news for many hands-on jobs.
