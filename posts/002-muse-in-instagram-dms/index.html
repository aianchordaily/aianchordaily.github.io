Instagram now has an AI agent. We tested it.

TL;DR Meta's Muse AI agent now works inside Instagram direct messages and can act on posts you share, catch you up on chats and organize saved posts. Walmart is also letting Muse shop Black Friday deals, with spending caps and approval controls you should switch on.

The story

Meta is bringing Muse, its AI agent, directly into Instagram DMs. According to TechCrunch, you can ask Muse about a post, place or product, get a recap of DMs you missed, or share a post and ask it to do something with it, such as adding a café to a trip plan. Muse can also sort saved posts and reels into Collections, for example turning a recipe video into a grocery list.

The rollout started Nov 24 on iOS and Android in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore. The saved-post feature starts in the US and Canada.

The same day, Walmart announced that Muse can help shoppers find products, compare options and track Black Friday deals, with Walmart helping build the cart after you sign in to your Walmart account. TechCrunch reports you can set spending limits, require approval before any purchase and review orders the agent places, and that Muse doesn't get your Walmart shopping history.

We tried it

We tested Muse on our own Instagram account on Nov 26. Instagram showed a "Meet Muse" card in the feed, and Muse appeared in the share menu. We shared a pasta recipe reel and asked for a grocery list; Muse wrote one from the recipe caption. After we connected Walmart, it built a 19-item cart ($29.94 before tax and fees) and asked before going to checkout. It swapped one spice for a different brand when the first choice had no price, and told us so. Meta's own connection screen warns that Muse may take unexpected actions and should be monitored.

Why it matters

Most people have never used an "AI agent", meaning AI that takes actions rather than just chatting. Putting one inside an app hundreds of millions of people open every day changes that overnight. It also means the safety controls stop being a niche concern and become something ordinary users need to understand.

What this means for you

  • It's opt-in help, not a takeover. Per TechCrunch, Muse doesn't post, send or share anything without your approval, and you can ask it to forget information or undo actions.
  • Try a low-stakes task first, like turning a saved recipe reel into a grocery list.
  • If you let it shop, set a spending limit and require approval before every purchase. Review orders afterwards.
  • Watch for scams. Expect fake "Muse deal" links this week. Open Muse from inside the app, never from a link.
