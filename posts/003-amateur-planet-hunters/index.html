Two hobbyists, free NASA data and an AI helper went planet hunting

TL;DR Two amateur astronomers used OpenAI's AI tools to search public NASA telescope data and produced dozens of planet candidates, one of which has gone to NASA's TESS team. They aren't confirmed planets, but the story shows how open data and AI assistants let non-professionals contribute real scientific leads.

The story

OpenAI describes how two amateur astronomers used Codex and the Astra model to search the public archives of NASA's Kepler and TESS missions. Both telescopes watch stars for the small, regular dips in brightness that happen when a planet passes in front of its star.

One hobbyist searched about 100,000 Kepler stars and flagged more than 200 signals. Ten of them were planets that had already been confirmed, which suggests the search method was finding the real thing. The other screened about 19 million stars and produced 49 candidates. An independent SETI Institute astronomer recovered two of the signals, and one candidate was sent to TESS researchers.

Why it matters

Finding planets used to require specialist software skills and a lot of time. Here, AI coding assistants did much of the heavy lifting: writing the analysis code, running it over huge datasets and helping sort real signals from noise. That lowers the bar for "citizen science," where volunteers help professionals with work there aren't enough experts to do.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, these are candidates: OpenAI itself says confirmation can take months or years. Second, one of the two hobbyists is an OpenAI engineer and the write-up comes from OpenAI, so it is also a product showcase rather than independent research.

What this means for you

  • You can contribute to science. Projects like NASA's Exoplanet Watch welcome volunteers, and AI assistants can help you learn the tools.
  • Public data is a gift. Many government and research archives are free to explore with AI help.
  • Read "AI discovered…" headlines carefully. Ask whether it's a candidate or a confirmed result, and who is telling the story.
