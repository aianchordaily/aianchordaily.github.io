Big News, Plain Words #006 · November 26, 2026

Your chatbot company went bust. Who owns your chats now?

TL;DR In Character.AI's bankruptcy, state attorneys general won a court-appointed privacy ombudsman to review any sale of user data, which Minnesota's attorney general says the company plans to auction on December 1. It's a reminder that what you tell a chatbot can outlive the company that runs it.

The story

Character Technologies, the company behind the Character.AI chatbot app, is in bankruptcy. According to the Minnesota Attorney General's office, a coalition of state attorneys general won a court order appointing a Consumer Privacy Ombudsman, an independent reviewer who must examine any sale of users' personal data. The Minnesota AG says the company plans to auction chat data on December 1, 2026, and puts the app's current user count at 12 million.

The attorneys general warn that a buyer might not keep the company's current limits on use by minors. (Reports differ on exactly how many attorneys general joined; Minnesota's office says 40.)

Commentators are already drawing wider lessons. 9to5Mac argues the episode shows why it matters where and how AI companies store your conversations in the first place.

Why it matters

People tell chatbots things they wouldn't tell most humans: health worries, relationship problems, money trouble, fantasies and secrets. Most never think about what happens to those words if the company fails. In a bankruptcy, customer data can be treated as an asset that's sold to pay debts. The ombudsman adds a check, but it is not a guarantee that the data stays private.

What this means for you

  • Postcard, not diary. Assume anything you type could one day be read by someone else.
  • Keep the most sensitive details out (health, finances, other people's private information) unless you trust the provider's data policy.
  • Use the controls. Most AI apps let you delete chat history and opt out of having your chats used for training.
  • Parents: if your teen used Character.AI, talk about what they shared and help them delete their history.
  • Follow-up: we'll report on what happens after the December 1 auction date.
