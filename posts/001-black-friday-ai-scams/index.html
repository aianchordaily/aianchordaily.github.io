Scam Alert #001 · November 26, 2026

That Black Friday deal looks perfect. That's the problem.

TL;DR Scammers are using AI to make fake stores, fake charities, fake product ads and even fake voices look and sound real this holiday season. A few simple habits block most of it, so you don't have to be able to "spot the fake."

The story

On the eve of Black Friday, the FBI warned shoppers that scammers are using AI to copy legitimate retailers' websites and to write emotional appeals for charities that don't exist (WGME). Forbes, citing the FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report, notes 56,478 complaints about items that were paid for but never delivered, with over $503 million lost, and 22,364 AI-related complaints with over $893 million lost (Forbes via Yahoo).

AFP reports a newer twist: AI-generated short-video ads pushing holiday products that don't exist, such as themed advent calendars and heated robes. H&M told AFP its name was used falsely in robe ads. One shopper paid over US$200 for four robes before the site went offline. Another paid US$280 for a "smart" Christmas tree and received only a fake invoice (AFP via The Star).

Voices are being faked too. Decrypt reports that a Port St. Lucie, Florida man was sentenced to 47 months in federal prison after using an AI-generated voice recording of the streamer N3ON to get about $260,000 in crypto sent to accounts he controlled (Decrypt).

Why it matters

For years, the advice was "look for typos and bad design." AI has wiped those clues away. Fake sites now look pixel-perfect, fake ads look professionally produced, and a voice note can sound exactly like someone you know. The defense has to shift from spotting fakes to habits that work even when you can't tell.

What this means for you

  • Go direct. Type the store's web address yourself or use its official app. Don't tap links in ads, texts or emails.
  • Pay with a credit card, not a debit card, wire, gift card or crypto, so you can dispute a charge.
  • Distrust urgency. Countdown timers, "only 3 left" and prices far below everyone else's are pressure tactics.
  • Check the tracking. A "shipped" email with no real tracking number is a red flag.
  • Vet charities on Charity Navigator, give.org or the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search before giving.
  • Set a family safe word. If a "loved one" calls or sends a voice note asking for money, hang up and call them back on a number you already have.
  • Report it at ic3.gov and ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
