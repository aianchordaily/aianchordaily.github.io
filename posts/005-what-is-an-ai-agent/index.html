Concept of the Day #005 · November 26, 2026

What is an AI agent?

TL;DR An AI agent is an AI system that doesn't just answer questions. It takes actions for you by using tools like a web browser, apps or files. Agents are now showing up in everyday apps, so it's worth knowing how they work and how to keep them on a short leash.

The story

For most people, "AI" has meant a chatbot: you type a question, it types an answer, and you decide what to do with it. An AI agent goes one step further. Give it a goal, such as "find me a cheaper flight" or "add this café to my trip plan," and it works toward that goal by taking actions.

Most agents run in a simple loop:

  1. Plan: break the goal into steps.
  2. Act: use a tool, such as searching the web, clicking a button, filling in a form, sending a message or editing a file.
  3. Observe: look at what happened.
  4. Repeat until the job is done, or stop and ask you when it's unsure or the next step matters.

The software around the AI that gives it tools and enforces the rules is often called the agent's harness. That's a future Concept of the Day.

This week's examples: Meta's Muse agent now works inside Instagram DMs, and Walmart lets it build a Black Friday cart with spending limits and approval before purchase.

Why it matters

Agents can save real time on boring, multi-step tasks. But an AI that can act can also act wrongly: buying the wrong thing, messaging the wrong person, or touching files it shouldn't. AI labs test for this. For example, Anthropic's new Siren evaluation checks whether agents stay inside the boundaries they're given.

What this means for you

  • Know which one you're using. Chatbot = advice you act on. Agent = it acts for you.
  • Keep approvals on for anything involving money, messages to other people, or deleting things.
  • Start small. Try low-stakes tasks first and watch how the agent behaves.
  • Review afterwards. Check orders, sent messages and changes the agent made.
